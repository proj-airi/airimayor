# Project city observations from recorded tools

The offline City page projects successful city reads, native statistics history, write ledgers, actions and returned images inside the existing recording worker. The city observation module exposes a cursor-scoped `view`; the host returns bounded chart points and paged readings/actions through the existing worker interface. A new periodic sampler was rejected because existing tools already record the city's facts and clock anchors, and another capture path would change what is observed and duplicate the persisted contract.

City observations are available only after their source record is reached. A native statistics response can reveal earlier samples at their original frames; it cannot reveal them before that response. Current cards use the last observation at or before the selected city frame and retain its date and evidence. Dates label exact recorded clock anchors; other native history frames remain explicit without inferred dates or filled values. Overlapping history samples use the latest acquired reading for the same metric/frame. Untimed reads remain inspectable in Evidence. Finance rates retain their monthly units, demand factors remain signed, and absent fields are not zero-filled. An exhaustive citywide notification count snapshot does observe zero for a previously seen type no longer present; its filtered and capped detail rows never replace those counts.

Road traffic series retain entity index and version, so changing or truncated road lists cannot become a citywide traffic average. Writes show their original before/after differences and the shared ledger judgment, including failed writes that changed the city. Clock advances use their completion anchor; other reads use their application anchor, or completion when application is unavailable. Images load on demand from the latest acquired map/screenshot at the selected city time. Choosing evidence seeks to its exact source record; closing city evidence restores the entry replay position. This extends [0054](0054-offline-recording-analysis.md): Metrics and Search cover the recording, while City follows acquired knowledge at the replay cursor.

City reuses AIRI's `TimelineRoot`, `TimelineAxis`, `TimelineLanes` and `TimelineCanvas` for the recording-time view, zoom, pan, scrubbing, keyboard navigation and one solid cursor. ECharts draws only the value plot and its value axis; its independent axis pointer and tooltip are removed. Hovering changes the inspected reading without moving or repainting the playback cursor. Plot data and value scaling are fixed until the selected measure or range changes; view changes update the library's horizontal extent. The recording module supplies a bounded overview separately from the acquired readings at the replay cursor. A second city-time slider and a separate chart interaction model were rejected because they diverged from Timeline.

The horizontal axis is recording time, shared with Timeline and the bottom playback control. Each recorded read contributes its latest city value at the time its result was acquired. Steps hold that recorded value until the next observation; they do not reconstruct the city's unobserved development. Native statistics history remains dated and inspectable in acquired reading rows and source evidence, rather than assigning invented replay times to historical samples. Full-recording previews are identified as such and never supply future values to current cards, actions or images. Long series retain local extrema; recent observation ranges are selected before sampling.

One playback module maps the shortened-gap clock to a continuous recorded-time cursor each animation frame, independently of asynchronous worker reads. Timeline, City and the main time slider consume that cursor; committed records still govern the available facts. Pause, resume, speed changes and scrubbing retain positions between records. Opening evidence keeps the return behavior described above. Population, treasury, monthly balance and notifications retain their original observation dates. Readings, actions, notification details, images and source evidence open on request, leaving the initial plot the full width. Traffic chooses a searchable road and then a measure, rather than flattening every road/measure pair into one menu.
